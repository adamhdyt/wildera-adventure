Operating mission-critical core banking systems requires more than theoretical database administration; it demands hands-on mastery over complex high-availability (HA) topologies, automated disaster recovery drills, and split-second incident responses.

As part of Bank IBK Indonesia's engineering talent development, I was selected for an intensive international database infrastructure training program at the **Industrial Bank of Korea (IBK) Headquarters in Seoul, South Korea**. 

This document summarizes the enterprise architecture patterns, automated recovery workflows, and production clustering principles explored alongside IBK's global infrastructure engineering leads.

---

## 1. The Global Banking Scale Challenge

IBK Korea supports millions of corporate and retail banking customers, processing billions of won in real-time transactions daily across Seoul, Tokyo, London, and international subsidiaries.

At this tier of operation, downtime is measured not merely in minutes, but in millions of dollars of financial exposure and regulatory penalties. The operational standard is strictly defined:
- **Zero RPO (Recovery Point Objective)**: Under no circumstance can a single verified financial transaction be lost, even during sudden data center outages.
- **RTO (Recovery Time Objective) < 15 Minutes**: Full service failover to disaster recovery infrastructure must occur within minutes.
- **Continuous Split-Brain Prevention**: Multi-node RAC clusters must cleanly arbitrate node evictions and fencing without data corruptions.

## 2. Deep Dive: Enterprise Multi-Node RAC Clustering

One of the central tracks of the program focused on advanced Oracle Real Application Clusters (RAC) 19c management on engineered systems and Oracle Linux.

### Key Focus Areas:
1. **Cache Fusion Interconnect Tuning**: Investigating interconnect latency between cluster nodes. Even sub-millisecond delays on private interconnect switches can cause `gc buffer busy acquire` and `gc cr multi block request` cluster wait events under concurrent OLTP workloads.
2. **Clusterware Fencing & Split-Brain Mitigation**: Configuring redundant Voting Disks across separate storage arrays and setting up IPMI fencing mechanisms to safely evict malfunctioning nodes without taking down the entire cluster.
3. **ASM Flex Disk Groups & Quorum Disks**: Implementing optimal redundancy with dedicated quorum disks to guarantee cluster quorum even during complete storage rack failures.

## 3. Disaster Recovery: Synchronous Far Sync & Active Data Guard

In banking, disaster recovery data centers are often located hundreds of kilometers away to guard against natural disasters or metropolitan power grid failures. However, network physics introduces a dilemma: synchronous redo replication over long distances introduces noticeable latency to core banking transactions.

At IBK Headquarters, we analyzed the deployment of **Oracle Far Sync instances**:

```
[Primary DC - Seoul] 
        │ (Zero-latency synchronous replication)
        ▼
[Far Sync Instance - Seoul Metro Edge]
        │ (Asynchronous high-bandwidth transport)
        ▼
[Disaster Recovery DC - Busan]
```

### Advantages of the Far Sync Architecture:
- The primary production database in Seoul commits transactions synchronously to a lightweight **Far Sync** instance located nearby on ultra-low latency fiber.
- The Far Sync instance ships the redo logs asynchronously to the distant DR data center in Busan.
- If the primary data center experiences a total catastrophe, the Far Sync instance flushes all remaining redo logs to the DR standby before failover, guaranteeing **zero data loss (RPO = 0)** without imposing network latency on live transactions.

## 4. Live Disaster Recovery Switchover Drills

A disaster recovery plan that has not been tested in live simulations is simply a theory. A major highlight of the technical program was participating in full-scale **Disaster Recovery Role Reversal Drills**:

1. **Pre-Switchover Validation**: Checking Redo Transport Services, archive log gap sequences, and verifying that the standby database has applied all redo logs to within zero lag.
2. **Automated Broker Cutover**:
   ```sql
   -- Validating readiness for role transition in Oracle Data Guard Broker
   DGMGRL> VALIDATE DATABASE 'BUSAN_STANDBY';
   DGMGRL> SWITCHOVER TO 'BUSAN_STANDBY';
   ```
3. **VIP & Application Redirection**: Testing automated client failover using Oracle Transparent Application Failover (TAF) and Fast Application Notification (FAN) events, confirming that connection pools redirected traffic with minimal connection resets.
4. **Graceful Reinstatement**: After simulating primary host recovery, reinstating the former primary as a standby using Flashback Database without performing a time-consuming full database rebuild.

## 5. Applying the Principles at Bank IBK Indonesia

The lessons learned from IBK Headquarters directly transformed our database administration workflows in Jakarta:
- **Standardized AWR/ASH Baseline Automation**: Implemented automated bash and PL/SQL monitoring packages that alert on anomalous wait-event spikes before users report latency.
- **Quarterly DR Readiness Drills**: Established structured, scheduled switchover drills to keep our local runbooks fresh and team execution sharp.
- **Enhanced Oracle 19c RAC Deployment**: Applied verified kernel and network buffer parameters during our recent core banking database upgrades, ensuring bulletproof stability and high availability.

Global perspective reinforces that great database engineering isn't about responding to disasters—it is about designing resilient systems where disasters become non-events.
