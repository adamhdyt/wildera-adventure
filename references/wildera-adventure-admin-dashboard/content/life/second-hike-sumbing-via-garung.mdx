- **Elevation**: 3,371 m (Puncak Kawah / Puncak Rajawali)
- **Route**: Garung Trail, Kalikajar, Wonosobo, Central Java
- **Elevation Gain**: ~1,850 m of steep volcanic incline
- **Timing**: May 2024
- **Crew**: 4 High School Friends (SMA brotherhood reunion)

---

## 1. The Call Returns: Leveling Up After Merbabu

There is a well-known truth among hikers: once you stand above the clouds, ordinary life never quite looks the same. 

My first mountain climb was Mount Merbabu back in July 2022 right after college graduation. That experience was raw, grueling, and eye-opening. But it also left behind an indelible itch—a quiet pull to return to the thin air, the heavy pack, and the honest camaraderie that only high mountains can demand.

Two years later, in May 2024, the call became impossible to ignore. Four of us who had been close friends since high school (SMA) decided to reunite. While most reunions happen over coffee or dinner in town, we wanted something that would strip away the noise of our day-to-day routines. 

We chose **Mount Sumbing** (3,371 masl) via the legendary **Garung route** in Wonosobo. Standing as the third highest volcano in Java (trailing only Mount Semeru and Mount Slamet) and paired as the iconic twin of Mount Sindoro, Sumbing is renowned for its steep, rocky grade and unpredictable mountain weather.

---

## 2. Garung Basecamp & Pos 1 Malim (+1,800 MDPL)

We arrived in Wonosobo in the early hours, greeted by the crisp mountain air carrying the rich scent of volcanic soil and scallion fields. Garung is the oldest and most historic trailhead on Sumbing. 

From the village basecamp, local *ojek* riders ferried us through the sprawling agricultural plantations up to the trailhead gate at Pos 1 Malim (+1,800 MDPL).

![Starting the trek at the Pos 1 Malim wooden signpost (+1,800 MDPL) via Garung with three high school friends as morning mist rolls through the trees.](/images/sumbing/sumbing-pos1-malim.webp)

At Pos 1, we hoisted our packs, strapped our waist buckles tight, and took our first team photo beneath the weathered wooden signpost. The mountain was draped in low-hanging mist, cool and quiet. We shared easy smiles and jokes, but we all knew that over 1,500 vertical meters of rocky ascent lay between us and the summit ridge.

---

## 3. Into the Mist: The Endless Incline of Garung

The Garung trail doesn't offer gentle warm-up stretches. Right from the start, the trail transitions from dirt paths into rocky stairways and deeply grooved volcanic gullies.

![Climbing through the highland forest trail, backpacks loaded and footsteps finding rhythm on the incline.](/images/sumbing/sumbing-forest-trail.webp)

As we pushed past Pos 2 and headed toward Pos 3, the surrounding canopy grew dense with gnarled, moss-covered trees. The weather turned cinematic—waves of thick mountain fog drifted through the forest, swallowing sound and giving the climb an otherworldly atmosphere.

![Stepping through a misty arched canopy of mountain branches, carrying the red Consina pack along the quiet ridge.](/images/sumbing/sumbing-misty-ridge.webp)

Hiking with high school friends brings an effortless comfort to the struggle. No one had to pretend to be superhuman. When someone needed water, we all paused. When the incline demanded hands-on-knees breathing breaks, we laughed about our burning thighs and shared chocolate bars and electrolyte drinks. The shared effort washed away years of distance within hours.

We pitched our tents at camp, sheltered behind natural earthen mounds against the notorious high-altitude winds of Sumbing. After a warm meal of hot soup and sweet tea under a freezing night sky, we rested our muscles for the midnight summit push.

---

## 4. Summit Push: Sindoro Above the Sea of Clouds

Our headlamps flickered to life at 2:30 AM. The final push up Sumbing's upper ridges—past *Pestan* and the steep boulder fields of *Pasar Watu*—tested every ounce of grip and stamina. The wind was relentless, and the temperature hovered in the single digits.

Just as our breath turned ragged on the final switchbacks, the sky began to bleed amber and gold.

We hauled ourselves onto the rim of **Puncak Kawah** at 3,371 MDPL. When we turned around, the sight stole whatever breath we had left.

![The majestic, symmetrical cone of Mount Sindoro rising triumphantly above an endless white ocean of clouds.](/images/sumbing/sumbing-sindoro-sea-of-clouds.webp)

Directly across the valley, **Mount Sindoro** stood bathed in morning light, a near-perfect volcanic pyramid rising above an unbroken, rolling ocean of white clouds (*samudera di atas awan*). Below the inversion layer, towns and roads were invisible. There was only blue sky, golden morning rays, and the twin peaks staring at each other in absolute stillness.

![Summit selfie with the crew at 3,371 MDPL, celebrating our ascent with Mount Sindoro towering in the crisp morning backdrop.](/images/sumbing/sumbing-summit-selfie-sindoro.webp)

![High school friends reunited at Puncak Kawah Gunung Sumbing (3,371 MDPL) with Mount Sindoro framed behind us.](/images/sumbing/sumbing-summit-friends.webp)

We gathered by the summit marker—*"Puncak Kawah Gunung Sumbing 3.371 MDPL"*—and celebrated. Standing there shoulder-to-shoulder with friends who knew you back when life was just homework and classroom desks, now standing at the roof of Central Java, was a moment of pure gratitude.

![Adam standing at Puncak Kawah Gunung Sumbing (3,371 MDPL), savoring the vast open vista and the silhouette of Mount Sindoro.](/images/sumbing/sumbing-puncak-kawah-adam.webp)

## 5. The Signature Adventure: Descending into the Active Crater

For the vast majority of hikers, reaching Puncak Kawah is the finish line. You take your photos, eat a snack, and prepare for the long descent. 

However, Mount Sumbing holds an extraordinary geographical crown: unlike mountains with narrow knife-edge peaks, Sumbing cradles an immense volcanic caldera basin known as *Kawah Sumbing* (or *Segoro Banjaran*). Looking down from the rocky rim, we saw trails winding down into the smoking caldera floor.

We looked at each other and nodded. We hadn't come this far just to look from the edge—we wanted to experience the inside of the volcano.

![Peering over the rugged crater rim down into the vast volcanic caldera basin, where sulfur steam vents hiss against towering rock walls.](/images/sumbing/sumbing-crater-rim-view.webp)

The descent into the crater bowl is steep and thrilling. We navigated down loose scree, volcanic gravel, and alpine boulders, carefully planting our boots with every step.

![Carefully scrambling down the alpine slope and yellow volcanic plains toward the smoking heart of the caldera.](/images/sumbing/sumbing-crater-descent.webp)

Stepping onto the crater floor was like landing on another planet. The scale of the caldera was breathtaking: a massive amphitheater of sheer vertical cliffs enclosing a flat volcanic basin with a seasonal turquoise tarn on one side and active fumaroles on the other.

![A breathtaking panorama inside Sumbing's caldera: a seasonal crater tarn nestled beneath sheer volcanic walls and billowing sulfur steam vents.](/images/sumbing/sumbing-hero-caldera.webp)

Plumes of dense, white sulfur steam billowed from vents in the rock face, hissing with the raw energy of the subterranean earth. The sharp, mineral tang of sulfur filled the air, and the sheer rock walls echoed with the deep rumblings of the mountain.

![Billowing sulfur steam vents rising against the towering, sheer rock face of the volcanic crater wall.](/images/sumbing/sumbing-sulfur-steam-vents.webp)

![Taking a quiet moment perched on a volcanic boulder in the heart of Sumbing's caldera floor, surrounded by immense crater walls.](/images/sumbing/sumbing-crater-floor-adam.webp)

Sitting on a volcanic boulder in the middle of the caldera floor was surreal. Looking up, the summit rim where we had stood just an hour earlier seemed distant and miniature. We were tiny specks inside the beating heart of an active volcano.

---

## 6. The Long Descent: Fog at Batu Belah and Shared Endurance

After exploring the caldera basin, we faced the arduous climb back up the loose scree to regain the crater rim and begin our descent to basecamp.

Descent on Sumbing via Garung is famously punishing on the knees. Gravity demands constant braking on steep rock steps and loose dust. By midday, thick mountain fog rolled back in, shrouding the entire trail in a heavy white blanket.

![Taking a pause by the historic Batu Belah trail signpost, completely enveloped in dense alpine fog during the descent.](/images/sumbing/sumbing-batu-belah-mist.webp)

At **Batu Belah**, visibility dropped to less than ten meters. The trail became quiet and meditative once again. Our legs were exhausted, our hiking boots were coated in fine volcanic silt, and our bodies felt every single kilometer. 

Yet nobody complained. The shared physical toll was bound by the high of what we had just witnessed together. By late afternoon, when we finally stepped off the trail back into the warmth of the village, we were utterly drained—and completely invigorated.

---

## 7. Reflections: What Sumbing Taught Us

Comparing my first hike on Merbabu to this second hike on Sumbing, each mountain imparted a distinctly different lesson.

Merbabu in 2022 was about transition and proving resilience—a cathartic closing chapter on university life and an entry into the corporate world. 

Sumbing in 2024 was about **depth, reunion, and curiosity**. It taught us not just to stop at the conventional milestone of the summit rim, but to take that extra step down into the crater—to discover what lies beyond the typical photo spot.

Most importantly, it proved that true friendships don't fade when life gets busy. You can go months without speaking, but the moment you put on your boots, share the weight of the climb, and stand together in the heart of a volcano, you realize that brotherhood forged in youth only grows stronger with time.

Until the next peak.
